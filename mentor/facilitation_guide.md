# Facilitation guide
## Before the meeting
Open the course start page, the Hebrew presentation (HTML or PDF), the Schroeder demo and notebook 00.
Test the projector and network. Ask students to check Google login before arrival.
Choose the Google account and the private program channel for submissions. Open the
recorded fallback if the external simulator is unavailable. Review the demonstration
record: an apparent ordered/disordered state is an observation, not an equilibrium label.

## During the hour
0–8: invite introductions and one reading question. Ask what a graph axis means and
whether they have used Python. 8–18: show research slides 1–6; invite a prediction before
revealing an example. 18–30: run only the guided demo. 30–50: everyone saves their own
notebook, runs two cells, edits an answer and reopens the saved copy. 50–60: walk through
Task 1 and Task 2, show submission steps, and check one practice notebook link per student.
If time is tight, shorten research discussion; retain save/reopen/share practice.

## After the meeting
Check that you can access the practice notebooks. Record next actions in a private
tracker. Give feedback on reasoning, not agreement with an expected answer. Invite
students to report confusing outcomes rather than silently replacing them. Arrange
follow-up before December 6. Use the rubric to identify one strength and one next step.
